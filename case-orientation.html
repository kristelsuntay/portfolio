Story-Driven New Employee Orientation for a Mining Company

A natural-resources company had its new employee orientation as a stack of slide decks. I turned it into a series of short Storyline modules linked by one storyline, with each topic matched to an interaction that suits it.

Client
Mining & natural-resources company (under NDA)
Audience
New hires, office and site-based
My role
Instructional designer
Tools
Storyline 360, PowerPoint, Excel

The problem

Orientation content lived in separate slide decks: vision and values, company profile, sustainability, safety, code of conduct and employee programs. It was a lot of information to give someone on their first week, and nothing tied it together or checked that new hires could use what they had read.

Approach

1. Objectives first

For each module I pulled the learning objectives from the client's source material and rewrote them as things a new hire can do, for example "recognize the steps to take when there is a fire or earthquake" or "distinguish the right behaviors of an employee." Every topic and activity was then mapped back to an objective.

2. One story across all modules

Instead of six separate lectures, the learner plays a new employee helping open a new mine site. Each module adds a piece: values guide a supplier decision, safety scenarios happen during a site inspection, and the course ends at a leadership meeting where the learner shares the experience. Quiz performance decides the outcome: the site either needs more preparation or is ready to open within a month.

3. The right interaction for each kind of content

ModuleContentInteraction chosen
Vision, Mission & ValuesCore valuesClick-and-reveal real company cases per value, then a scenario question
Company ProfileStructure & operationsMap overview, org-level graphic, click-to-reveal division leads
Sustainability (ESG)Environment, social, governance goalsAnimated timeline with milestones; click-and-reveal SDG contributions
Safety OrientationFire, earthquake, site safetyBranching scenarios ("an earthquake hits during an inspection") and a drag-and-drop: equip the worker with the right PPE
Code of ConductTerms, sanctions, proceduresAlways-on glossary button, guided conversation with a colleague, step timeline
Employee WellnessEngagement programsPhoto highlights and the story's closing scenario

The rule behind the choices: practice what learners will do, reveal what they only need to know. Safety and conduct, where wrong choices have real consequences, got scenarios. Reference content got lighter click-to-reveal and timeline formats.

4. Sized to fit the budget

I scoped each module as an Essential or a Comprehensive package (about 3 to 12 minutes of eLearning), plus optional 3–5 minute microlearning videos for key topics. That let the client choose depth module by module and see the cost of each option.

5. Detailed storyboards and a reusable template

The storyboards specify more than the text on screen. They include voiceover scripts, the exact order and timing of animations, and the client's photo and color rules, so a developer can build each slide without guessing. I also built a branded Storyline template (title, objectives, topic menu, content layouts, knowledge check and closing screens) so every module looks and works the same.

Storyboard excerpt (animation spec):
Fade in the slide title (0.5s). After a 0.5s pause, the vision statement appears line by line (0.7s each). Hold 1s, then fade in the call to action. At the same time, a subtle pulse highlights the photo gallery and loops until the learner clicks it.

What I delivered

  • Module and objectives map with topic-by-topic interaction plans and time estimates
  • Storyboards with voiceover scripts and animation specs (Vision & Values, Code of Business Conduct, Whistleblowing)
  • Branded Storyline 360 template and style rules for icons, colors and photography
  • Scoping and cost estimate for the eLearning (SCORM) and microlearning video packages
